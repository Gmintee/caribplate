At Divali, Maintain Your Calorie Goal

Divali is about light, family and food. Nobody should spend it counting every bite. But if you are working towards a daily calorie goal, a few simple habits let you enjoy the spread and still feel on track the next morning.

Think in days, not plates

Your goal is for the whole day, not for one meal. If you know the big meal is coming, plan the rest of the day around it. Eat a normal, filling breakfast and keep lunch light and simple. Skipping meals to “save up” usually backfires, because you arrive hungry and eat faster and more than you meant to.

Start with the savoury food

A traditional Divali meal is vegetarian, and much of it is naturally filling and good for you. Fill your plate first with the dishes that carry the meal: dhal, pumpkin, bhaji, channa and aloo. Paratha and rice come next, in sensible portions. By the time you reach the sweets, you will be satisfied rather than starving.

Watch the extras that add up quietly

  • Fried items such as pholourie, baiganee and saheena soak up oil.
  • Sweet drinks count too. A large glass of juice or sweetened drink can match a small dessert.

Choose your sweets on purpose

Divali sweets are the highlight, and they are small and rich. Decide in advance which two or three you really want, and skip the ones you can eat any day. Take one piece of each, sit down, and enjoy it slowly. Peera, ladoo, gulab jamun, jalebi and barfi are rich, so one piece goes a long way.

A sample plate

Here is one plate that comes to roughly 950 calories.

  • 1 small serving of paratha roti (about 4 oz)
  • ½ cup dhal
  • ½ cup pumpkin
  • ¾ cup channa and aloo
  • ½ cup bhaji
  • A spoon of tamarind or mango sauce
  • 1 small sweet, such as a gulab jamun or a small portion of peera

Fine print: The calorie count is an estimate. Actual calories vary with the recipe, ingredients, cooking method and portion size.

Sharing the load when you visit

Divali often means visiting several homes, and each host will want you to eat. Take a small portion at each one rather than refusing or filling a plate every time. It is polite and it keeps your day in balance.

If you go over, carry on

One big day does not undo a good week. Do not punish yourself by skipping meals the next day. Go back to your normal eating, drink water, and carry on.

Divali comes once a year so eat well and enjoy the company.
